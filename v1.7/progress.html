The eight pieces, and what round 1’s critics said

Eight builders worked in parallel, one per piece, meeting only at the shared context and the event bus. Eight blind critics then scored each piece against matched reference frames. Seven came back behind; one came back close.

shell-onfoot behind

The house and the workshop you walk through, the descent, the rack, picking an armour.

The basement’s darkness is empty, not full. The lighting recipe is right — pools, cool practicals, black falloff — but there is almost nothing for the pools to land on. On a 5-bucket luminance histogram the reference workshop puts 22% of its pixels above the bottom fifth with a broad 13% low-mid band; ours put 3% and 1%. Root cause is volume, not light settings: 52 × 56 m holding ~24 objects against a reference that packs ~50 readable objects into 30 m². The monitors were flat pastel fills where the reference carries dense blue-white UI.

armors-classic behind

Mark I, Mark II, Mark III — the built ones.

The bodies are stacks of axis-aligned primitives with bevels, not anatomical shells. The reference upper arm swells at the deltoid and tapers to the wrist; the thigh swells at the quad and necks into the knee. Ours are constant-diameter cylinders shoulder-to-wrist and hip-to-ankle, a box pelvis, and world-axis-aligned rectangles laid onto the tube. Dead-symmetric silhouettes, no plate overlap, no dark gap under any plate — nothing in the outline says “separate pieces of armour”.

armors-modern behind

Mark XLII and Mark L — the nanotech ones.

Surface detail painted onto a body that is not a body. Measured on the silhouettes, Mk L is 0.665 / 0.673 / 0.685 / 0.672 of height wide at shoulder / chest / waist / hip, and Mk XLII 0.701 / 0.699 / 0.709 / 0.717 — under 3% variation from armpit to hip. No deltoid cap, no pectoral mass, no lat spread, no waist, where the reference reads as a shoulder span ~1.4× the waist with a hard V. No hands on either armour, mitten feet, a smooth egg for a helmet crown.

suitup behind

The five suit-up rituals: gantry, flying plates, nano wave, and the faceplates.

The Mk III gantry beat — the piece’s own headline test — does not read as Iron Man. At 40% through, the body is a bare t-shirt and jeans with only boots attached: no red/gold shell, no mechanical core, no wiring, no chest reactor light, and gantry arms rendering as flat beige plastic cylinders holding nothing. The reference is mirror candy-red and gold hung on chains over bundled coloured wiring, lit hard against near-black.

flight-hud behind

The 6DOF flight model and the JARVIS visor.

A competent flight-sim overlay, not JARVIS. Against the film frame: median luminance 15/255 with 57.5% of pixels below 20, versus ours at 73/255 with only 8.3% below 20 — the visor interior is five times too bright. Structurally the film draws zero lines that span the frame (four irregular, off-axis, partly-defocused clusters around a large black centre); ours draws a full-width heading tape, a full-height altitude tape, a full-height right-hand table and a full-width bottom bar — a symmetric picture frame, plus a 12-key legend strip the film has no equivalent of.

world behind

Malibu, the cliff, the ocean, the town, the fair, the donut.

Nothing in the outdoor world has a surface. Terrain, mountains, ground planes, the shop, the house and the ocean are flat-shaded vertex colour with at most a bump map. Measured on the matched cliff frame: pixel-scale detail (mean deviation from an 11px box blur) is 3.45 ours vs 9.71 reference on the cliff face, 3.39 vs 5.99 on the plateau, and the cliff face is half as bright (56 vs 110). More low-frequency variation than the plate and far less high-frequency — sub-metre form has to come from maps, not vertices.

destruction behind

Repulsor bolts, fracture, debris, the things that break.

The repulsor is the only light in the shot, so the beat reads as a magenta blob instead of Iron Man. In the reference the palm flare is a small hard blown-out white region on a suit already legible in candy red and brushed gold. In ours the relationship is inverted: flashLight floods torso, abdomen and both thighs pink — a lit chest panel samples RGB (99, 0, 43), green channel exactly zero. Fired over bright midday ocean there is no bolt visible against the water at all, only two flat pink kite shapes where the flash sprites clip the forearms.

audio close

The whole soundtrack: thrusters, wind, servos, glass, the reactor, music.

The one piece that is close. The remaining hole is the wind bed, and it owns the signature moment. At 290 m/s it measures a spectral centroid of 424 Hz, 95% rolloff 840 Hz and 0.73% of energy above 6 kHz, and it stops brightening — 175 → 211 → 315 → 424 → 424 Hz across the speed range. Cause is a mismatch: wind_high.mp3 is cut from a domestic electric fan (centroid 1543 Hz) while the mixer sweeps that layer’s highpass up to 7370 Hz, far above anything in the recording, so the layer climbs out of its own material.

Integration bugs found and fixed in this pass

None of these belong to a builder. Every one of them lives where two pieces meet, which is exactly the code no builder owns and no critic scores.

The suit took off in the wrong room

suitup ↔ flight
What was happeningsuitup:done called flight.activate() with no position, and activate() falls back to world.spawn — the living room. Measured: the ritual finished on the gantry pad at (−6, 91, 14) and the very next frame put the player hovering at (4, 99, 18), eight metres up in a room he had never walked into. Then he could not get out: eight seconds of full vertical thrust from there gained one metre of altitude, because he was under the roof.
Fix, and the measurement after itThe armour’s root is at the soles and the flight model is a point mass one metre above them (FEET_OFFSET), so the handover now reads the suit’s own world transform and lifts it by exactly that metre. Nothing moves at all. Take-off is measured at (−6, 92, 14) — on the pad, for all five armours.

Malibu stayed lit like a basement after take-off

onfoot ↔ env ↔ flight
What was happeningThe environment mix is measured off the boy’s feet inside onfoot._step, and that function returns early the moment he stops being a boy. Nothing else in the game drives ctx.env, so the workshop’s night lighting froze on and stayed on: measured env.current = ‘workshop’, k = 1 while the suit flew over the cliff in daylight. combat.js carried a hack that forced ‘exterior’ whenever you fired, which is how it stayed hidden.
Fix, and the measurement after itThe env rule was split out of the walk into envFollow(point) and the suit is handed to it once the boy is inside the armour — same rule, same function, different point. The mix now crossfades workshop → exterior over about two seconds as he climbs out of the house: measured k = 1 → 0.41 → 0 at 0 / 1 / 2 s of climb.

Picking an armour left him grinding against a tool chest

world ↔ dress ↔ onfoot
What was happeningChoosing a mark walks the boy onto the gantry pad, and the walk was a straight line. The pad is 19 m away across a floor that world/ fills with benches and dress.js fills with chests, crates, drums and a car; neither knows the walk exists. Measured: from the Mk I case he covered 5 m, hit the corner of a chest at (−12.2, 8.9), and ground against it at 0–0.5 m/s for 9.5 seconds until the 13-second approach timeout gave up and teleported him onto the mark. All five armours ended on that timeout.
Fix, and the measurement after itThe walk now plans a route instead of guessing one: a breadth-first search on a half-metre grid whose edges are tested with the same sweep the mover uses, string-pulled down to a handful of corners. It costs 37 ms, once, on the frame he commits. Walk times measured after the fix: Mk I 5.4 s, Mk II 10.2, Mk III 10.2, Mk XLII 4.0, Mk L 5.8 — none of them on the timeout. A stall detector fans the wish direction sideways as a backstop, and dress.js now keeps the pad’s row clear.

The flight model shoved the suit up through the basement ceiling

world ↔ flight
What was happeningworld.surfaceHeight() is the outdoor surface: inside the house footprint it returns the main slab at 96 m whether you are standing on it or in the workshop five metres below. So the first ground pass after take-off pushed the armour from y = 91 straight to 97 — through the slab, into the living room, on the frame the faceplate closed.
Fix, and the measurement after itAnything more than a body-length below the reported surface is indoors, not falling through terrain. Ground resolution now stands down there and lets the room’s own colliders do the work until the suit climbs out.

Restart left you flying

engine shell ↔ flight ↔ suitup
What was happeningR respawns the boy in the living room, but nothing told the flight model or the suit-up module. Pressing it at 51 m/s over the Pacific put the boy on the sofa and left the camera, the armour and the whole JARVIS HUD out at sea, on a game state that said mode: ‘onfoot’. A restart during a ritual was worse: the sequence kept running against a player who was no longer there, and the next armour you picked did nothing at all.
Fix, and the measurement after itflight and suitup both listen for restart now — the model deactivates, the sequence cancels, the stage lights go down, the gantry stows and the armour leaves the world. Verified: restart from 51 m/s over open water returns a clean on-foot living room with no HUD.

The sound is real recordings, not a synthesiser

This is a standing requirement on the build, so it gets re-proved every pass rather than asserted. Every sound in the game is a decoded file that tools/fetch-audio.sh downloaded from a permissively licensed source and audio/CREDITS.md credits by name, licence and URL. The bank refuses to make a waveform: a missing file leaves a null buffer and the play() on it becomes a no-op.

The biggest gaps going into round 2

Ranked by how many of the eight critiques each one would close at once.

Bodies

armors-classic, armors-modern, suitup

Three critics named the same thing from three angles: the armours are proxy tubes. Shoulder-to-waist variation under 3%, no hands, no jaw. No shader work moves this — it is the mesh. It is also the single change that most improves the suit-up beat, the rack, the hover shot and the combat shot at the same time.

Surfaces

world, shell-onfoot

Nothing outdoors has a map. Measured sub-metre detail is a third of the reference on both the cliff face and the plateau. Indoors the same shortfall shows as an empty room rather than a flat one: the light is correct and has nothing to fall on.

The visor is a picture frame

flight-hud

Five times too bright inside, four full-frame axis-aligned tapes where the film draws none, and a keyboard legend the film has no equivalent of. This is the cheapest of the three to fix and it is in every single flight frame.

The wind stops brightening

audio

The only gap on the piece that is already close. wind_high is a domestic fan cut whose material ends around 3.6 kHz, and the mixer sweeps its highpass to 7370 Hz — so past the middle of the speed range the layer contributes about 1% of the bed and the wind gets darker as the suit accelerates. It needs a brighter recording, not a different curve.

Still open, and deliberately not touched

Found in this pass, left alone because fixing them would mean redesigning somebody’s piece rather than repairing a seam.

Landing on water is landing in water

Descending onto the sea at (0, −60) settles the suit at y = −1 with altitude 1, because surfaceHeight there is below sea level and nothing consults oceanHeight. Land on the terrace and it is correct.

Floating crates in the workshop

A few dress.js crate stacks sit in mid-air where the bench they were meant to sit on is not. Visible in the arrival frame, left alone because it is the dressing author’s call, not an integration seam.

MZ.shot() still doubles the HUD at any size but the live one

Carried over from the previous pass and unchanged. Every screenshot on this page was therefore taken with MZ.screenshot() at the live canvas size, which is always clean.

suitup:plate, footstep, break and repulsor:hit have exactly one listener each

They fire and they land, so nothing is broken, but each is one accidental deletion away from being silently dead. Worth a second listener or a contract test.

Reference & architect

Reference library assembled under reference/ — film stills, armour photography, HUD frames, the Malibu house, Randy’s Donuts. Stack fixed: vendored three.js r169 + Rapier3D WASM, no build step, Blender headless for every model. registry.js frozen with eleven module stubs so the game boots at every moment of the build.

shell-onfoot behind · armors-classic behind · armors-modern behind · suitup behind · flight-hud behind · world behind · destruction behind · audio close. Every verdict backed by a measurement against a matched reference frame, not by taste.

Round 1 — integration, first pass

Played the whole loop headlessly. Four dead wires between pieces found and fixed — the faceplate had no key bound at all, every repulsor shot panned to dead centre, falling rubble was silent, and four downloaded interface sounds were unreachable. One screenshot-tool defect and three smaller items documented and left open.

Round 1 — integration, second pass (this one)

Drove the loop again after the first pass and found that the middle of it was broken in five more places, all of them at seams: where the ritual hands over to flight, where the boy hands the environment to the suit, where the dressing meets the walk, where the outdoor heightfield meets an indoor room, and where restart meets everything. All five fixed and re-measured. The core loop now runs end to end on a clean load, for all five armours.
